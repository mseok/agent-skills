# Journal profiles

Checked 2026-09-10. Recheck the named journal's current official guidance before claiming submission compliance. Do not transfer a Nature main-journal rule automatically to Nature Communications, another portfolio journal, Extended Data or a supplementary PDF. Follow specific production instructions when supplied.

## Nature main-journal final artwork

Official sources:
- https://www.nature.com/documents/nature-final-artwork.pdf
- https://research-figure-guide.nature.com/figures/preparing-figures-our-specifications/
- Initial-submission comparison: https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/initial-submission

The final-artwork PDF specifies 89 mm single column, 183 mm double column and optional 120/136 mm intermediate widths. Ordinary text is 5–7 pt, preferably Arial or Helvetica; panel identifiers are lowercase, bold, upright, 8 pt. Keep text editable, unoutlined and fonts embedded in PDF. RGB is recommended; vector PDF is a suitable deliverable.

The online figure guide specifies labelled axes with units, accessible colors and no overlapping text. It gives 300 dpi as the photographic minimum and 450 dpi in its export section. Use at least 450 effective dpi for newly rendered image components where practical; disclose low-resolution source limitations instead of upsampling to claim compliance.

Guidance is not internally uniform: the initial-submission page gives 90/180 mm widths and 170 mm maximum height, while the final-artwork PDF lists a 247 mm full page depth. Do not call either height a universal limit. Use 170 mm as a conservative local working cap when no production instructions exist, reserving caption space; state the chosen stage and source. Do not mix widths from different stages silently.

## Local defaults, not publisher mandates

Use 7 pt ordinary labels and ticks/keys; use 6 pt only when genuinely needed for dense content, 8 pt panel letters and Arial when available. For a user-requested larger-type style variant, 8 pt titles/axis labels with 7 pt ticks are an available local override; disclose that this exceeds the strict Nature ordinary-text limit. Use about 0.5–0.75 pt axes/keylines as a starting point and increase emphasis selectively; confirm any target journal's line-weight rule. Choose height from content rather than enforcing a fixed aspect ratio. Compact panels may use a single column; mixed multipanel figures often need two.

Use an accessible categorical palette with stable semantics, sequential maps for ordered nonnegative values and diverging maps only around a meaningful reference. Retain accepted project colors if distinguishable. These are design choices, not an official Nature palette.

## Other targets

For Nature Communications or any other journal, retrieve its own artwork/author guidance and record a separate profile with source/date, widths, height constraints, fonts, panel labels, line weights and output requirements. If retrieval fails, produce a clearly labeled style draft with stated provisional parameters; do not assert compliance. Do not invent a universal caption word limit.
