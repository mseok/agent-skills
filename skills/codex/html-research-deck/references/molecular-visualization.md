# Molecular figures for research talks

When the user asks for a protein structure, realistic molecular view, ligand-bound complex or pocket, prefer coordinate-based rendering in PyMOL over generative illustration. A realistic-looking generated protein is not a structure.

- Reuse the project’s PyMOL environment when available. Otherwise install PyMOL in a separate environment rather than altering the presentation runtime. Use a suitable available structural viewer if PyMOL is unavailable, and retain equivalent reproducible settings.
- Obtain a relevant experimental PDB/mmCIF or user-supplied coordinate model. Check species, chain, ligand, mutations, missing regions and method. Label predictions as predictions. If no appropriate structure exists, state the gap and use a simple explanatory diagram rather than fabricate coordinates.
- Render a restrained academic figure: white/transparent background, protein cartoon, ligand sticks, selective side chains or a subtle surface only where they clarify the question. Show an overview and/or pocket close-up as needed. Avoid glossy, inflated molecular surfaces and decorative atoms.
- For comparisons, align protein backbones and keep a common view and scale. Preserve each deposited ligand pose relative to its protein. Do not dock, remodel or add a hypothetical bound substrate unless that operation is requested; a nucleotide analogue is labelled as an analogue, not catalytic ATP.
- Compute contacts from coordinates, identify backbone versus side-chain atoms, and distinguish proximity from evidence of a hydrogen bond. Do not infer affinity from a distance or crystal structure. Label all added mechanism arrows as explanatory, with editable PPT text and arrows outside the structural rendering where practical.
- Derive displayed chain, residue name/number and atom labels from the same selected coordinate records used for each distance. Carry these labels with the measurement into the slide and citation map rather than retyping residue identities from memory; a correct residue-number selection can still acquire the wrong residue name in manually written prose.
- Retain source coordinates, PDB/DOI, chain/ligand selection, render script, PyMOL session/view, output image and relevant measurement/overlay settings. Put concise citations on the slide and full provenance in notes. Deliver image assets as raster renderings without claiming their internal elements are PPT-editable.

## Make cited interactions locatable

When a residue or contact is part of the slide's argument, the audience must be able to locate it in the structure without inferring it from a distance list below the image. Select and highlight that residue from the actual coordinate records, use a distinct consistent color (retaining conventional atom colors where useful), and place a readable residue label immediately next to the visible site. A native editable text label may overlap quiet protein cartoon space; use a thin leader to the selected atom/residue when its target is ambiguous. Avoid covering the ligand, contact dashes or key atoms. The label color and highlighted residue should correspond; color alone is insufficient.

For the GSK3B comparison, identify Val135 in each panel, highlight the relevant backbone N/O geometry, and place `Val135` or `Val135 · 주사슬` beside the highlighted hinge region. A cyan fragment without a local label does not establish where Val135 is. Apply the same principle to Cys87 or other residues used as evidence, rather than reserving it for this one residue.

Choose a pocket crop/scale where the ligand, highlighted residue and interaction lines remain legible at normal slide size. Add a close-up inset when the required overall structure view makes the site too small. Retain the common aligned orientation for comparisons. Record the residue/atom selection, highlight color and label/leader placement with the render assets. Verify final slide/PDF appearance after inserting the images; a clear PyMOL window alone is insufficient.

## Crop, edges and scale in the finished slide

The structure is usually the evidence band's main object, so it must fill ≥ 55 % of the band at slide scale. Render at ≥ 2400 px on the long side with a white background, then auto-trim the white margin (about 2 % padding) before placing it with `containRect()` so its visible bounds, not its canvas, define the placement. Do not let the image boundary slice through a cartoon ribbon: show the whole domain, or restrict the cartoon to residues within ~10–12 Å of the ligand so the boundary is a natural fragment edge, and inspect all four edges at full size. Keep the ligand and highlighted residue large enough that their labels are readable at slide scale (zoom to the pocket; use an overview inset only when needed). Compared panels share view, scale and frame size; label each residue with `figureLabel()` + `leader()` placed on the visible site.

RDKit drawings: highlight along bonds (`highlightBonds` plus small atom radii, about 0.2–0.3), not large atom bubbles; keep atom labels (HO, NH2) clear of bond lines (adequate `padding`, `bondLineWidth` ≥ 2); place compared molecules in the same orientation and scale, and draw them large enough that heteroatom labels read at slide scale.

3D pocket context: slab/cut-away renders leave black cut faces; show the hinge strand or a thin backbone tube around the ligand plus labelled residues instead, and say what the cropped view is. Check `check_layout_quality` fill for line-art/3D slides after enlarging the structure rather than adding decoration.

`figureLabel()` has no background by default: a white box behind a label cut off a ring-oxygen atom label (and, earlier, a hinge strand). Turn `halo` on only for a verified blank area and inspect the crop at full size.

Draw dashed contact lines only for donor–acceptor-compatible pairs (backbone N–H···O, N···N with a known donor/acceptor role); never an acceptor–acceptor O···O or C···O pair as a “hinge contact”. Every dashed line on a slide appears in the notes with its atom names and distance computed from the same coordinates, and a dashed line that is not in that list is a defect.

PyMOL foreground clutter: hide the cartoon of residues in front of the ligand's rear plane but keep the hinge segment (list them in a JSON beside the assets so the view is reproducible) instead of slab cuts. One bond length and line width for every structure in a deck (the `draw_chem.py` defaults, 140 / 6): r20 slide 4 vs 6/8 differed 2× in bond width and label size and a judge saw two styles. `scripts/draw_chem.py` (run with an RDKit python) draws a structure the way the user approved on 2026-10-01: normal black bonds and coloured heteroatoms, with the shared scaffold/substructure shown only as a pale gray-blue halo drawn first. A saturated cyan scaffold with peach halos was “too prominent”; do not recreate it. It prints the PNG size and atom pixel positions for `figureLabel()`/`leader()`.
