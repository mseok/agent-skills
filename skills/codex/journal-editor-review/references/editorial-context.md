# Evidence for editorial opportunity

Editorial reasons to Send need the same scrutiny as scientific criticisms. Read this when the Editorial track is requested: it governs Editorial A/B and the final adjudication, and Critical readers consult the relevant evidence when it changes their judgments. "Mandatory" below means mandatory within that track.

## Recent publication precedents

Search the **same journal and article type over the 2–3 years preceding review_as_of** by topic and contribution form. Include relevant methods, integrations, resources, benchmarks and discoveries without transferring another contribution type's validation requirements. Label adjacent types/older precedents separately. Later citations, policies or editor rosters cannot explain an earlier judgment; any requested current-opportunity update is separate and dated.

Use a bounded purposive set of representative papers, stating selection logic and coverage gaps. For each: title/DOI/URL, publication date/type, what it contributed, evidence actually read, similarities and differences from this manuscript, and the implication for journal interest. Read relevant full-text Methods/results where the comparison depends on them; mark abstract-only access. Do not require an arbitrary paper quota or silently call a few examples an exhaustive journal survey.

For the citation comparison cohort, specify contribution/type/field, time window and age eligibility **before inspecting citation outcomes**. Do not select papers because they are conspicuous citation successes. If counts/fame were already known or convenience selection was unavoidable, disclose outcome-aware selection rather than claiming blind selection; check equally eligible less-cited or uncited alternatives when accessible. There is no mandatory high/low quota, and a sparse or fully high-cited eligible cohort is not automatically invalid. Bound its representativeness and forecast confidence. Preserve the original eligibility logic when tracing the handoff; transparent provenance alone does not remove sampling bias.

Assess **active interest and saturation** together. Repeated coverage can show reader demand, or make another similar contribution insufficiently distinct. Compare the remaining capability/insight, competing alternatives and publication timing; explain which interpretation is better supported. Accepted-only examples do not reveal private rejection thresholds, causally prove editor preferences, or establish a typical rejection probability.

## Mandatory early citation and IF assessment

Explicitly answer: **Could an editor reasonably expect this manuscript's early citations to contribute to the journal's citation performance and maintenance of its IF?** Also assess long-term field influence. These are separate reasoned judgments, not an official paper-level acceptance rule or a numerical citation forecast.

Collect/report:

1. **JIF baseline:** latest verifiable Journal Impact Factor **publicly available by review_as_of**, with value, metric year, official source, retrieval date and release/public-availability date when needed. Retrieval today does not prove historical availability; a dated release can establish it. Prefer Clarivate or official publisher metrics. Distinguish JIF, CiteScore, five-year JIF and release year; leave unverified values unknown, not filled from memory. If an official source confirms no JIF has been assigned, report that fact/source and mark maintenance of an existing IF **not applicable**; inability to find a value is **unverified**, not proof of non-assignment. Keep citation and long-term assessments in either case.
2. **Comparable citation observations:** apply predeclared eligibility and similar publication ages. A recent relevance cohort and older citation cohort may differ. Identify comparator, window/age, count definition/source/retrieval date, and cutoff. Exclude citing events after review_as_of; do not treat a later complete first-year count as observable before that year elapsed. Use an actually observable shorter window or older comparators, labeled clearly. Use a consistent source or explain coverage differences.
3. **Who would cite and why:** name communities and concrete follow-on paper uses (direct method use, baseline comparison, resource/benchmark reuse, hypothesis follow-up). Link these to manuscript affordances and observed precedents; popularity of AI alone is not evidence of demand.
4. **Diffusion and constraints:** plausible user base, setup/compute/validation cost, code/data access and licensing, reproducibility, transfer to other problems, and competing standard tools/resources. An inaccessible or expensive workflow may limit adoption even when scientifically useful.
5. **Time and uncertainty:** give separate **high / medium / low / insufficient evidence** ratings for early citation outlook and long-term impact, each with its strongest favorable evidence and strongest counterevidence. Explicitly answer the mandatory IF-contribution question with its own evidence status when different; an early-citation outlook from proxy observations is not automatically evidence of JIF-specific contribution. No new score or numerical forecast is required. Explain what extra data would change the judgment. Citation counts are observations, potential uptake is inference, and an editor's IF-related motive remains an inferred possibility unless explicitly public.

If data are missing, keep this section, state what is unverified and give the best bounded assessment/verification path; insufficient evidence is valid. Unknown IF is not an automatic veto, and unsupported high ratings are not allowed.

**Policy governs use, not whether the assessment is reported.** Verify what the target's policy actually excludes: a ban on JIF as an individual-paper quality proxy is narrower than a ban on forecast-citation-led editorial selection. Apply only the stated scope. When policy excludes that forecast from selection, keep the mandatory IF/citation assessment as diagnostic context, but do not use it to determine or tip the policy-consistent Send/Desk recommendation. Report contrary private-incentive hypotheses separately as unverified speculation, not hidden requirements. Permitted reader relevance can still be assessed on independent evidence. Unknown policy establishes neither a prohibition nor permission to invent a citation threshold.

### Citation evidence handoff and final judgment

Editorial B passes a compact evidence basis with its full report: closest comparison papers and selection reasons, database/count definition, observation period and elapsed publication age, retrieval date, source locations, and the favorable/opposing arguments behind its outlook. Preserve unknowns and distinguish early citation contribution from long-term influence. This working-context handoff does not require a persistent database.

The final Editor receives and checks this basis. It may add, replace or correct sources, cohorts or windows, but must identify what changed, why, and the effect on interpretation, outlook and confidence. Keep original and revised provenance traceable; identical medium ratings do not establish identical evidence, and a changed source does not by itself require a changed rating.

The final Editor owns the answer: who would cite for which concrete use, how early prospects compare with the justified cohort, and whether this supports spending external-review resources. Do not simply copy Editorial B's label or interpret weak evidence as medium prospects. Lower confidence or use insufficient evidence when warranted while retaining the mandatory assessment. JIF remains journal context, not a paper-level citation threshold.

### Bibliometric interpretation

JIF is a **journal-level calendar-year ratio** using citations to items from the preceding two publication years; its metric year and JCR release year differ. It is not each article's first 24 months and not a requirement that a paper receive at least JIF-many citations. New-article effects depend on future windows and the journal's publication mix; do not calculate an expected change to JIF without the needed numerator/denominator data.

Current total citations or calendar-year bins do not establish first-12/24-month counts. To claim a specific post-publication window, use dated citing works or valid historical snapshots, state the publication-date convention and account for missing dates. Otherwise label approximate age-aligned observations or insufficient evidence. Database metadata changes and coverage vary. OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar counts are not the Web of Science/JCR numerator and do not reproduce a JIF calculation.

## Plausible handling team or route

Read official editorial structure, editor biographies and subject remits, relevant collections/editorials, and public handling acknowledgments when available. Prefer a plausible **team/subject area**. Name an individual only when a source specifically supports relevance; attach role/remit, source/date and why it fits. State that actual assignment is unknown unless explicitly established.

When two routes are natural, explain which manuscript contribution each route would emphasize or question, using documented remit rather than imagined personality. Distinguish professional in-house teams, academic boards and handling editors; a general portfolio description cannot override the named journal. Never invent personal tastes, private assignment, assignment probabilities or a secret IF target. If public evidence supports only a broad field, retain that precision and label individual attribution insufficient evidence.

## Provenance and failure handling

Keep source, observation and inference separate for each decision-critical positive or negative point. Record retrieval date and what was actually accessible (direct page, official indexed extract, partial paper or unavailable record). Do not bypass authentication or require credentials merely to complete a field. Use authorized accessible official alternatives and mark the residual uncertainty. Missing numerical/editor data do not authorize dropping their mandatory report sections.
