# Journal discovery

Read this before selecting the panel. Build a small source-backed profile in the working context, not a new database or journal installation.

## Resolve the target

- Expand abbreviations against the official publisher site. Confirm the journal title and publisher; distinguish a specific Nature title from Nature or a generic portfolio profile. If plausible identities remain, ask with the official candidates, a justified recommendation when possible, and alternatives.
- Infer article type from the supplied manuscript and request. Check the target's actual types and special issues; Methods, standard research, Resource, Application Note, Database and Web Server routes can have different criteria. Treat the inference as provisional and ask only if the distinction is consequential.
- Keep the requested assessment stage explicit. This workflow assesses pre-submission referral readiness, including revised manuscripts reassessed for that purpose; it does not silently substitute Send/Desk for a requested post-review acceptance/revision judgment.
- Establish **review_as_of** before collecting the profile. Current readiness defaults to the user's current date; retrospective work uses the requested historical cutoff. A manuscript's version date is not automatically its review date. Keep source retrieval time separate from when information became public. Ask with recommended options if an ambiguous date changes the task.
- For retrospective diagnosis, establish the submitted version and date. Use literature, policies, metrics, citation observations and editor remits available by review_as_of, and archived policy when accessible. Label current policy as current if historical rules cannot be verified; if only a current proxy can be verified, offer a clearly separate current/proxy assessment rather than presenting it as historical evidence. Do not cite a later competitor as a cause of an earlier rejection.

## Read primary sources

Prioritize official Aims & Scope, editorial selection criteria, author instructions for the article type, and reviewer guidance. Read relevant editorial explanations when they clarify what the journal values. Use recent, closely related published studies and public review files as examples, not a representative sample of all submissions or a known rejection threshold.

Record only decision-relevant fields:

| Field | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Identity and type | Official title, publisher/site, relevant article route |
| Requested decision and eligibility | Pre-submission referral question; any consequential official submission condition, evidence/status and whether it can be remedied |
| Governing time | review_as_of, manuscript version/date, and source retrieval/public-availability dates kept distinct |
| Reader and scientific context | Whose problem is addressed and the appropriate comparison community |
| Contribution | New method, useful integration, scientific finding, data/resource, application, replication/refutation, or another supported contribution type |
| Selection criteria | Explicit advance, relevance, evidence and reporting requirements |
| Process and materials | Internal screening, specialist consultation, referee stage and applicable information-sharing rules |
| Provenance | URL, page/section, access date, version/date when supplied, and important gaps |

For every consequential criterion distinguish:

- **Official requirement:** explicitly mandatory for this journal/article type.
- **Official editorial criterion or explanation:** a stated consideration requiring judgment.
- **Observed example:** a property of selected published papers, not a universal requirement.
- **Inference or unknown:** our interpretation, or information not recovered.

Do not fabricate weighted scorecards, reviewer-personality distributions, internal priorities, acceptance probabilities, or a venue-specific requirement for wet-lab validation, clinical deployment, algorithmic novelty, open data, or a fixed number of baselines. Apply the actual contribution and access context. Technical soundness, novelty, importance, and journal fit are separate judgments.

If an official eligibility or integrity condition is consequential, verify its applicability and the current evidence. Report administrative readiness separately from scientific merit/referral; a remediable submission condition is not by itself a scientific flaw. Unknown compliance is not proven misconduct. Established evidence-integrity problems may also affect specific scientific claims, with that link explained.

## Appropriate comparison context

Map the question, input, method, output and claimed benefit before searching nearest work. A broad fashionable field can make a useful advance look trivial; an artificially narrow comparison can make a familiar result look unique. Include pertinent non-agent and domain methods. A combination of known tools can be valuable, but its value and evidence must be assessed against the target's criteria rather than an automatic approval or rejection rule.

For decisive overlap claims, use the existing research-review novelty checks: inspect actual Methods/SI and evaluations, distinguish feature existence from solved capability, and retain version or access conflicts. A missing result from a search is not proof of novelty.

## Work identity and publication relationship

When a prior item's status could change originality or the verdict, establish author relationship, public form, and relation to the current manuscript. Distinguish a same-work version, a separate earlier work, an expanded follow-up and an unresolved relationship. Check whether workshop material is archival proceedings, an abstract, a non-archival presentation or a public manuscript; do not guess from the word workshop.

Treat the same work's preprint and later versions as one work/version family, not independent competitors or duplicate evidence against the author's own contribution. Shared authorship alone does not establish same-work identity: a separate study by the same authors can still limit the incremental contribution. Verify actual content overlap independently of authorship and publication form; non-archival status does not erase a substantiated scientific overlap.

Consult the target journal's prior/duplicate-publication policy only when eligibility is genuinely at issue. Keep scientific overlap, work identity and publication-policy compliance distinct. Unknown status alone is not a reason to deduct contribution or allege duplication; retain the specific uncertainty and any independently verified overlap. This is a decision-critical check, not a census of every cited author's publication history.

## Process fidelity

Follow the target's published process instead of assuming all journals use the same editorial board. Internal editors decide whether external review is warranted; external reviewers supply scoped technical/conceptual advice. The coordinating Editor retains judgment and explains how specialist evidence affects it. Check what the target says about cover letters, transfer history, anonymity, review sharing and revised manuscripts before passing those materials between roles. Do not impersonate named editors or infer their personalities.

A diagnostic audit after a simulated desk decline helps the author check the first impression. Label it as our additional diagnostic step, not a claim that the real journal necessarily performs that audit.

## Access limits

Use accessible official alternatives or a supplied official extract when a page fails. Record the gap; do not silently substitute a similarly named journal or portfolio-level policy for a verified journal-specific rule. Do not claim a paywalled paper or unavailable supplement was read. When the missing criterion controls the verdict, withhold that venue-specific verdict and ask for the smallest useful source while continuing independent scientific checks.
