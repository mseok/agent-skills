# Published article decisions

Complete main narratives and opening sections were read on 26 November 2026. These notes describe editorial choices, not a scientific replication or a journal-wide template. SI and code were not examined. Paragraph labels are analytical. Use the source links to inspect the original context; transfer decisions, not sentences.

## Deep active optimization for complex systems

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00858-x#Sec1) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-025-00858-x)

**Paragraph roles:** P1 societal purpose; P2 experimental cost/active learning; P3 AO definition; P4 surrogate choice; P5 reinforcement-learning mismatch; L1 data scarcity; L2 noncumulative rewards; P6 tree-search gap; P7 pipeline; P8 evaluation ladder.

The opening starts at societal benefit, then narrows to expensive nonlinear search. Citations support applications, active learning and competing methodological families. The gap accumulates data, dimensionality and reward constraints. AO is positioned in P3 before the literature review finishes; DANTE's formal contribution arrives in P7. P8 previews three evaluation tiers with numerical gains. Its lexicon links “noncumulative objectives” and “surrogate model” to “exploration–exploitation”. Qualifications appear in Results, including BO's advantage on small problems; the single Discussion paragraph instead expands into future applications. The optimization promise is tested, but forecasts of routine adoption remain aspiration. This is evidence that broad openings and long introductions occur, not evidence that they are necessary or that every published claim should be emulated.

## Less can be more for predicting properties with large language models

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-01045-2#Sec1) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-026-01045-2)

**Paragraph roles:** P1 success/failure tension; P2 data abstraction; P3 scientific stakes/question; P4 diagnostic contribution; P5 practical consequence.

The opening juxtaposes LLM success with elementary failure. P2–P3 introduce a reusable data abstraction and physical examples before asking whether coordinates are learned. Citation clusters support the tension and existing modelling traditions; the gap concerns what predictive success actually demonstrates. P4 states the negative finding and controlled-test logic; P5 previews architecture-selection implications without a metric inventory. “Coordinate–category”, “inductive biases” and “sample-efficient” connect computational diagnosis to materials prediction. The Discussion repeats the strong claim, then explicitly restricts model families, properties and scaling regimes. Results also report uneven scaling improvements. Thus, its architecture-selection promise is maintained, while categorical rhetoric needs reading alongside the experimental boundaries. The diagnostic contribution is substantive without introducing a universally superior model.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.

## Machine-guided path sampling to discover mechanisms of molecular self-organization

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-023-00428-z#Sec1) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-023-00428-z)

**Paragraph roles:** P1 mechanism → timescale mismatch → paired bottlenecks → solution.

The opening asks how molecular organization works and motivates the study through a timescale mismatch. One paragraph contains MD's promise, rare-event difficulty, the paired sampling/information gap and the algorithmic response. Five citations occupy conceptual hinges; detailed theory follows in Results. The ending previews capabilities and acceleration, leaving application enumeration and numerical comparisons for later sections. “Transition paths”, “metastable states” and “mechanistic models” make the computational obstacle physically specific. Discussion broadens applicability through a committor-like concept, then addresses state definitions, path duration and experimental validation. This preserves the introduction's two-part promise while placing conditions on generality. The observed compression works because the causal bottleneck is explicit; it provides no warrant for prescribing one paragraph to other contributions.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.

## The high-dimensional space of human diseases built from diagnosis records and mapped to genetic loci

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-023-00453-y#Sec1) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-023-00453-y)

**Paragraph roles:** P1 ontology challenge/prior networks; P2 representation proposal/embedding analogy.

The opening challenges conventional disease categories rather than advertising dataset size. P1 groups earlier work by evidence and network construction; P2 moves directly to continuous representation, explaining its analogy with word embeddings. The gap is a change in representational scope rather than an absent prediction algorithm. The introduction previews downstream uses and dimensionality, reserving cohort and association counts for Results. “Disease embedding”, “etiological discovery” and “disease constellations” bridge machine learning and biomedical interpretation. Discussion immediately acknowledges modelling decisions and incomplete histories, and qualifies what proximity may mean. Results substantiate representation and association analyses; treatment-selection suggestions go beyond the reported tests. This illustrates an economical conceptual introduction whose evidence scale becomes concrete later, without requiring a standalone gap paragraph.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.

## Large language models as uncertainty-calibrated optimizers for experimental discovery

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01283-z#Sec1) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01283-z)

**Paragraph roles:** P1 selection bottleneck; P2 BO limitation; P3 LLM limitation; P4 integration gap/contribution; P5 mechanism/examples; P6 selective benchmarks; P7 broader inference.

The opening converts rapid AI generation into an experimental-selection bottleneck. P2–P3 contrast BO's feature dependence with LLM unreliability; clustered citations support this argument rather than a chronological survey. P4 locates the gap in coupling the components. P5 explains the training mechanism and chemical organization; P6 selects cross-domain gains; P7 marks the broader interpretation as suggestive. “Marginal likelihood”, “latent space” and “sample-efficient” connect mechanism to discovery. Discussion returns to the same integration promise, then identifies GP scaling and structurally rich inputs as boundaries. The results sequence combines broad benchmarks with diagnostics, supporting the promised two-level account. The introduction's priority language is an author claim, not an independently verified priority assessment; demonstrated scope remains the evaluated chemical tasks.

## Molecular set representation learning

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00856-0#Sec1) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00856-0)

**Paragraph roles:** P1 disciplinary reciprocity; P2 representation critique; P3 hypothesis/requirements; P4 architectures/evaluation/benchmark warning.

The opening presents reciprocal relevance between molecular science and graph learning. P2 challenges representation assumptions through chemically specific bond examples and integrated citations. P3 explicitly hypothesizes that sets may help, then states architectural requirements and available precedents. P4 introduces architectures and previews both performance and benchmark criticism without a numerical catalogue. “Atom invariants”, “permutation invariance” and “molecular topology” carry the disciplinary argument. Uncertainty appears before contribution naming and recurs in Results as competing explanations: representation value versus dataset limitations. The Conclusion returns to both the methodological and diagnostic promises. The argument therefore cannot be reduced to a simple superiority narrative: modern datasets complicate the early result, while set-enhanced graphs remain useful. This is a hypothesis-led introduction with productive internal qualification.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.

## Meta-designing quantum experiments with language models

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01153-0#Sec1) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01153-0)

**Paragraph roles:** P1 field motivation; P2 isolated-solution gap; P3 meta-design contribution; P4 software analogy; P5 transformer precedents; P6 synthesis precedents/difference.

The opening combines quantum unintuitiveness with technological promise. P2 integrates precedents across design settings and identifies their isolated-solution limitation. The contribution appears in P3; P4 translates it into software terms; P5–P6 then resume related work and distinguish the purpose of generated programs. There is no terminal numerical scorecard. “Meta-solution”, “construction rules” and “general design principles” link representation to scientific understanding. Results separate successful, unexpected and failed generalizations; Discussion specifies numerical-parameter and data-generation constraints. Thus the family-level design promise is investigated without universal success. Cross-disciplinary future applications remain proposals. This counterexample matters: contribution-first positioning followed by further literature is a coherent observed sequence, and the generic field opening is not uniquely concrete.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.

## Echo state graph neural networks with analogue random resistive memory arrays

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-023-00609-5#Sec1) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-023-00609-5)

**Paragraph roles:** P1 data/hardware/training pressures; P2 hardware opportunity/failure; P3 co-design solution; P4 demonstrations/efficiency preview.

The opening starts with graph learning's reach, then quantifies data growth and training expense. P2 presents resistive memory's physical promise and its update-cost/stochasticity obstacle, with a dense supporting citation cluster. P3 turns that obstacle into a resource through co-design; P4 previews datasets and numerical efficiency gains. “Random projections”, “in-memory computing” and “backward pass” connect device physics and algorithmic costs. The qualifier “projected” accompanies efficiency comparisons in the introduction and Results; node classification is explicitly simulated. The one-paragraph Discussion restates the paired hardware/software promise and offers a future platform interpretation, without a dedicated limitations passage. This supports preserving measurement-versus-projection distinctions rather than inferring that every Discussion must have the same length or subsection structure.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.
