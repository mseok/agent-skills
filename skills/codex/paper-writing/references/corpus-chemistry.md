# Published article decisions

Complete main narratives and opening sections were read on 26 November 2026. These notes describe editorial choices, not a scientific replication or a journal-wide template. SI and code were not examined. Paragraph labels are analytical. Use the source links to inspect the original context; transfer decisions, not sentences.

## JCIM-1 — Real-World Molecular Out-Of-Distribution: Specification and Investigation

[Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10865358/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.3c01774)

**Paragraph functions:** P1 decision stakes and generalization; P2 intervention alternatives; P3 selection limitations; P4 design alternatives and disappointments; P5 diagnosis, protocol, investigation, preview.

The opening grounds importance in molecular scoring decisions before introducing an abstract distribution problem. Literature is organized by what an intervention changes: reliability assessment, data, selection, or model design. The unresolved issue becomes specification and evaluation, rather than another model architecture. Vocabulary links objects to operations: *molecular scoring*, *applicability domain*, *specify*, *evaluate*, *generalize*. Editorially, the useful pattern is a diagnostic question with an operational test; its necessity should not be inferred merely from the authors' diagnosis.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.

## DD-1 — Cost-informed Bayesian reaction optimization

[Primary source](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/dd/d4dd00225c) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4DD00225C)

**Paragraph functions:** P1 interaction example; P2 BO and procurement assumptions; P3 contextual costs; P4 proposed mechanism; P5 neighboring approaches; P6 qualified novelty and evaluation.

A concrete reaction-parameter interaction establishes the problem before Bayesian optimization appears. The gap is an assumption mismatch: experimental cost changes with laboratory inventory. The framework is introduced before the detailed related-work comparison, which groups methods by objective, constraints, context, and cost treatment. Vocabulary includes *inventory*, *ligands*, *acquisition function*, *update*, *balance*. Editorially, this is a useful mechanism-first comparison; generalized safety, sustainability, or scheduling benefits should retain their prospective status.

The opening explains why the predicted quantity matters physically and computationally. ## DD-3 — Operator-free HPLC automated method development guided by Bayesian optimization

[Primary source](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2024/dd/d4dd00062e) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4DD00062E)

**Paragraph functions:** P1 analytical stakes; P2 separation requirements; P3 established models; P4 data models; P5 automation limits; P6 transferable precedent; P7 search limits; P8 BO rationale; P9 neighboring Bayesian work; P10 closest comparator/contribution; P11 design principles/platform.

This application paper opens with impurity detection, resolution, and analysis time. Its longer Introduction builds a domain-specific chain of comparisons: retention modeling, data-based prediction, closed-loop flow chemistry, and competing optimization strategies. The gap is autonomous impurity scouting under practical chromatographic criteria. Vocabulary remains concrete: *chromatogram*, *baseline resolution*, *impurities*, *separate*, *optimize*. Editorially, the consequential distinction is between demonstrated automation and comparative algorithmic efficiency; their evidence should remain separately traceable.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.

## DD-4 — FSL-CP: a benchmark for small molecule activity few-shot prediction using cell microscopy images

[Primary source](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/dd/d3dd00205e) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/D3DD00205E)

**Paragraph functions:** P1 imaging applications; P2 cell painting; P3 activity-prediction stakes; P4 sparse-label problem; P5 few-shot formalization; P6 transfer mechanism; P7 adjacent paradigms; P8 contribution-list lead-in; bullets: dataset, baselines.

The opening establishes a measurement modality through its applications, then narrows to prediction with sparse assay labels. Subsections separate the biological task from the learning formulation. Literature is grouped by modality, application, and learning regime; the contribution list promises a resource and comparative baselines. Vocabulary includes *morphological profiling*, *support set*, *auxiliary tasks*, *transfer*. Editorially, this is a community evaluation contribution whose unresolved question concerns learning across sparse tasks; its comparisons should not be rewritten as proof that one input modality or learning paradigm is universally superior.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.
