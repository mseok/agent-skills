# From research notes to an Introduction argument

This is an independent synthetic teaching example, not a real study or a source of factual claims. Its purpose is to show how a supplied scientific purpose guides contribution selection across a whole argument. The same record can support different emphases; a diagnostic hierarchy is one possibility, not the default transformation of a method paper. Do not borrow its wording, scientific setting or paragraph structure.

## Supplied record

A hypothetical microscopy study develops adaptive selection of fields of view. Low-resolution images estimate where a rare cell state is likely to occur; high-resolution acquisition is then allocated among fields. In the supplied comparisons, rare cells are detected more often at the same total imaging exposure when they are spatially clustered. Uniformly distributed rare cells show no corresponding gain. Prior adaptive acquisition methods already select regions with salient visual features. The record contains no live-animal or clinical results and no experiment isolating each software component.

## An unresolved outline

Rare cells matter. Microscopy produces large data. Existing acquisition methods have limitations. The new system includes an image encoder, a selection rule and a feedback step. Several datasets show improved detection, although one setting does not. More validation is needed.

This outline can become fluent prose without explaining the contribution. The method inventory replaces an organizing idea, and the distribution-dependent result appears as a qualification of a general improvement claim.

## A method or capability purpose

Suppose the author aims to develop a useful acquisition procedure for observing rare cell states under limited imaging exposure. Preserve that purpose. The argument can move from the difficulty of finding rare cells, through the value of provisional spatial information, to a procedure that concentrates high-resolution acquisition where those cells are likely to occur. Credit prior adaptive selection and identify the actual empirical or methodological increment; the record does not support claiming that adaptive acquisition itself is new. The fixed-exposure comparison establishes the procedure's usefulness for clustered cells, while the uniform-distribution result defines a consequential condition. Neither result requires replacing the method's purpose with a study of its controls.

> Here we develop an adaptive imaging procedure for detecting rare cell states at limited imaging exposure. Low-resolution images guide high-resolution acquisition toward fields likely to contain the sought cells. At the same total exposure, the procedure increases detection of spatially clustered rare cells, while uniformly distributed cells show no corresponding gain.

## A diagnostic purpose

If the author's question instead concerns when adaptive acquisition is useful, the distribution-dependent finding can organize the argument. Prior work establishes that adaptive selection is possible; the remaining question concerns how the spatial distribution of the sought cells affects its value. The argument can develop from the need to observe rare cell states, through their organization across a specimen, to allocation of limited exposure. The method connects coarse localization with focused acquisition, and the result preview answers the distribution question. The null result is central because it helps answer this purpose, not merely because it is an informative control.

> Here we examine how the spatial distribution of rare cell states affects their detection by adaptive microscopy. Coarse images guide the allocation of high-resolution acquisition among fields of view, concentrating imaging exposure where the sought cells are likely to occur. At fixed total exposure, this strategy increases detection when rare cells are spatially clustered, with no corresponding gain for uniformly distributed cells.

Both passages retain the contrary observation and explain the method's role without attributing the benefit to an untested component. Their different openings and literature sequences would develop the scientific purpose each is meant to serve. A useful method or empirical advance need not establish a new general principle; neither purpose licenses hiding its novelty limits. The complete Introduction would explain the actual increment and cite the relevant prior work. These excerpts are not ending templates, and software components and acquisition parameters remain available for Methods.
