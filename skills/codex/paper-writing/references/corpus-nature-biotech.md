# Published article decisions

Complete main narratives and opening sections were read on 26 November 2026. These notes describe editorial choices, not a scientific replication or a journal-wide template. SI and code were not examined. Paragraph labels are analytical. Use the source links to inspect the original context; transfer decisions, not sentences.

## Nature — Accurate structure prediction of biomolecular interactions with AlphaFold 3

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07487-w)

**Paragraph functions:** P1 utility and accumulated progress → P2 scope question and comparator limits → P3 contribution and qualified comparison → P4 enabling architecture.

The opener is an abstract utility claim linking complex structures to biology and therapeutics. Prior advances build readiness; an explicit question converts success into scope expansion. Citations cluster around method families, including a concurrent generalist. Verbs describe operations—predict, replace, accommodate—around complexes, interfaces, coordinates, and diffusion. A productive decision is to explain what enables the expanded scope immediately after announcing it.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.

The productive decision is to connect a computational transfer mechanism to a biological constraint.

## Nature — Discovery of a structural class of antibiotics with explainable deep learning

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06887-8)

**Paragraph functions:** P1 stakes, projection, alternatives, gap → P2 prior successes, definitions, substructure hypothesis.

Opens with a societal threat, then attaches a projected mortality statistic and a compressed taxonomy of discovery approaches. The projection supplies urgency; it is not independently verified here. A second long paragraph links the authors’ previous hits to an explanatory limitation, defines technical concepts, and ends in a substructure hypothesis. A productive choice is to make the desired unit of discovery—a class of compounds—the reason explainability matters.

## Nature Biotechnology — Large language models generate functional protein sequences across diverse families

**Paragraph functions:** P1 approach taxonomy, limitations, sequence opportunity → P2 analogy and contribution → P3 learning and adaptation bridge.

The opener makes a broad application claim; a long first paragraph groups design approaches by information source, names limitations, and turns abundant sequences into an opportunity. ## Nature Biotechnology — Efficient evolution of human antibodies from general protein language models

[Primary source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-023-01763-2)

**Paragraph functions:** P1 search burden → P2 plausibility proposal and objection → P3 answer and test system → P4 method and input requirements → P5 experimental scope and efficiency.

Opens with an evolutionary search problem and converts its scale into laboratory cost without a combinatorial number. The next paragraph proposes sequence plausibility while confronting a serious objection: plausible variants might rarely improve the particular function. The answer arrives in paragraph three, before method setup and a selective experimental preview. Lexicon centers on mutations, fitness, selection pressures, affinity, and maturation, with guide, enrich, recommend, and improve. The productive choice is to state the uncertainty that makes the experiment informative before announcing its answer.

*Reading scope:* Complete main narrative and opening read.
