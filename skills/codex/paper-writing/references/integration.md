# Integrate the paper around one central message

Run this pass after drafting the parts and before final sentence polishing, including when different writers produced different sections. Use the agreed one-sentence central message from the plan or the author's latest framing. It states what the reader should newly understand, with the scope supported by the evidence. When no framing is agreed, infer a provisional message from the supplied manuscript and evidence; do not silently replace an author's decision.

## Read the argument in the actual draft

Make a reverse outline from what was written, not what the original plan intended: summarize the new understanding contributed by each core paragraph in one sentence. Name its role in the central argument, such as posing the question, explaining the approach, supplying evidence, distinguishing explanations or bounding a conclusion. Supporting paragraphs can name what they support; routine Methods details serve comprehension and reproducibility without restating the central claim.

Read these sentences in order. Check what the reader learns next, why that information belongs here, whether prerequisites arrive before their use and whether the space given to each result matches its importance. Independent tests can stand in parallel. Do not force every result into one causal chain or add repeated slogans to manufacture coherence. Within the authorized scope, repair roles, order, repetition and proportion before polishing transitions.

## Check the sections together

- The Introduction establishes the question and promises an advance that the Results actually address.
- The Methods explain how the approach and comparisons answer that question, with the information needed to interpret and reproduce them.
- The Results build an answer through complementary findings. A later analysis or case adds understanding beyond another score or win.
- The Discussion explains what that answer changes, at the strength the evidence supports. The abstract and title, when supplied, make the same scoped claim.

Read the Introduction's closing argument, the Results headings and core findings, and the Discussion's opening and conclusion together. Their subjects, claim strength and conditions must agree, even when their wording differs. A contrary result helps define the central message's valid scope; do not remove it to make the story smoother. If the evidence requires changing an agreed headline or scope, follow the revision workflow rather than silently rewriting the author's framing.

## Select and finalize

Use the contrasting examples and limitation-selection rule in `register.md` to check what earns space in the prose. Preserve essential evidence while combining repetition and placing supporting detail where it serves the reader. Then polish sentences and complete the skill's factual and prose checks.

For a single-section task, apply the pass to the supplied section and surrounding context. Do not expand the edit scope, invent missing sections or claim whole-paper alignment without reading them. Flag a material mismatch outside the authorized passage instead of rewriting it. Keep the reverse outline as working material, not manuscript prose or a mandatory extra deliverable.
