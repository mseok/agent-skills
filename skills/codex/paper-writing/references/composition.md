# Compose the paper

Read the author's original message and authorized writing references, the brief, the complete research facts, and the complete named published openings. Include corrections and contrary findings. Preserve the scientific meaning and binding author decisions while choosing the prose and organization. The message states what the paper should communicate; it need not be the Introduction's first sentence. When no message is supplied, use the provisional one in the brief.

Develop that message through the background, prior work, explanation and evidence the intended readers need. Use literature to establish premises and credit existing accomplishments. For a method, explain its organizing idea through the scientific difficulty or decision it addresses. Give controls the weight their findings warrant in that argument. Learn from the named openings' reasoning, explanatory depth and disciplinary usage while writing this paper's own progression.

For an Introduction, normally establish the consequential scientific problem, then narrow to the question, approach and answer. Introduce a particular system or comparison when its significance is intelligible; it can open the paper when it supplies that significance itself and fits the author's direction. Choose paragraph boundaries, explanatory depth and result detail from the argument, without a fixed template or quota. A useful empirical or methodological advance can carry a paper without a new general principle.

Write in the field's language: name scientific objects, actions and consequences. Organize observations into findings and explain what the measurements mean when the record permits. State findings with their meaningful conditions and keep consequential exceptions beside the claims they change. Let an established setting carry through a connected passage. Preserve units, comparators, uncertainty and the distinction between observation and interpretation. Use only supplied facts and named sources; do not invent missing support. Record derived values separately with their operands, units and basis. Use the specified citation placeholder or author issue for a consequential gap.

Write the complete requested passage, then read it continuously for meaning and progression. Make the question, contribution and answer intelligible in the same terms; repair missing premises, abrupt changes of subject and repetition. Follow the requested format and length. For scoped revisions, preserve the authorized passages and decisions and mark proposed changes inline. Return the requested prose and actionable author issues; keep planning and verification records outside the manuscript. The coordinator owns the subsequent factual and full reference checks.
