## Authority and units

Source: `20260526_SeokhyunMoon_Dissertation.{key,pptx,pdf}` under `/Volumes/ExternalSSD/work/projects/thesis`. Keynote is the user-identified authoring original. PPTX has 107 slides; PDF has 84 pages with different order/content. Match by title and content. Do not infer scientific recency from file modification time.

PPTX size: 24,384,000 × 13,716,000 EMU. Keynote coordinates: 1920 × 1080 points. Artifact Tool uses CSS pixels: multiply source point coordinates and fonts by 4/3 for a 2560 × 1440 canvas. Do not retain original font sizes while accidentally halving slide dimensions.

## Visual rules

- White background. Actual research visuals dominate, with no generic decorative artwork.
- **One font family for every editable run (titles, body, citations, chart labels, tables, Hangul, Latin, Greek α β γ ρ μ, Å, digits): Pretendard** (SIL OFL; user choice 2026-10-01: Mac + Windows, English + Korean, one clean font). It is not preinstalled on either OS, so `createDeck()` and `finalize.mjs` run `scripts/ensure_font.py`, which installs the bundled `assets/fonts/Pretendard-{Regular,Bold}.otf` into the user's font folder automatically when missing (user-level, no admin rights; the user approved this on 2026-10-02; `--check-only` just reports). Machines that only open the deck need the same files (`assets/fonts/README.md`); nothing is embedded in the PPTX. `setFamily('Pretendard')` is the kit default, `finalize.mjs --family Pretendard` the default policy (it also sets the theme fonts), and the kit shifts node labels −2 pt (`FAMILY_OPTICAL`) because Pretendard's line box sits low. Re-run `check_font_glyphs.py` on the final deck. Only on request: Apple SD Gothic Neo single, Helvetica Neue (English-only Mac decks) or `pair_fonts.py` (Helvetica Neue + Apple SD Gothic Neo; the checkers accept exactly that pairing). Preserve labels inside original paper images as source artwork rather than claiming those fonts are editable.
- Left margin 95 pt, width 1730 pt. Gray section label at y≈49, 30–32 pt; title at y≈85, 73–85 pt bold; gray subtitle at y≈187, 40 pt.
- Evidence usually occupies y≈290–900. Short interpretation at y≈940, ≈40 pt, often centered. Avoid repeating the title.
- Small gray page number centered near x≈960, y≈1030.
- Source divider accent #1F77B4. Gray baselines; restrained green/orange/purple only for meaningful distinctions.
- Simple centered section dividers and generous whitespace. Never inherit the committee, date or publication claims on new covers.
- Use concise topic, method and evaluation titles throughout, matching the dissertation's subject-first headings. Per the user's 2026-09-14 correction, avoid completed-action or claim-style result titles such as “We screened…” or “All inhibitors rank…”. Explain findings in the body and short bottom interpretation. Preserve a good supplied title. A selected case does not prove general superiority.
- Current user defaults override historical template formatting: body text and multiline labels use **1.2× line spacing** (`lineSpacing: 1.2`), not 1.2 pt of absolute line height or character spacing. This interprets the user's “글들 간의 간격 1.2pt” feedback as a line-spacing multiplier; follow any later clarification. Resize/reflow text boxes to fit rather than compressing lines. Keep gaps between separate blocks deliberate; do not apply 1.2 pt as every object gap.
- External references go in the lower right at the apparent size of **Helvetica Neue 20 actual PPT points**: Pretendard at **20 pt** (its x-height is slightly larger, so never go below 20; Apple SD Gothic Neo needs 20.5 pt), using compact author–journal–year or APA style. Keep full bibliographic details in notes. Reflow references and reserve footer space rather than shrinking below the chosen visual size. Use `Lowry et al.`, not `Lowry 등`, including in inline Korean citations. Do not add “This study” or a self-citation to original analysis/results.
- Use a **single native arrow preset** such as `rightArrow` for a solid process arrow, or a native connector with an arrowhead for linked nodes. Do not construct arrows from a rectangle plus a separate triangle. Grouping those pieces does not make them a native arrow. Keep connectors attached to the relevant objects.

## Text inside diagram shapes

Use a node's own native text body for short molecular labels such as GSK3B, ATP, CHIR or a phosphosite. Set horizontal alignment to `center`, vertical alignment to `middle`, symmetric left/right and top/bottom insets, and zero paragraph spacing before/after. The style-kit `box()` helper applies these properties and names the node. Do not place a separate left/top-aligned textbox on a blank shape to imitate its label. Use the same helper for related nodes so normal and inhibited states share typography and alignment.

An overlapping ligand shape must not cover or displace the protein label. Reposition/resize the actors or provide a separate clearly defined pocket region; do not move only the GSK3B text off-center to avoid the overlap. Every label stays optically centered in its own visible container. Native arrows/connectors should connect the intended actor boundaries.

Inspect the exported PPTX and native/PDF rendering at slide scale and a close view of the diagram. Balanced settings do not guarantee optical centering across renderers; check actual text position, multiline height, clipping and overlap. Correct the container/insets coherently rather than adding spaces or a manual text overlay. **Node labels use zero horizontal inset** (the kit default): Apple's QuickLook renderer shifts centered text right by the left inset (probe: +18–20 pt with 18 pt insets, 0–2.5 pt with none); give the box ≥ 16 pt free width on each side of the longest line instead. **Node labels use line spacing 1.0** (the `box()` default); a multiline label gets its 1.2× look from 0.2 em of paragraph spacing before each later line, which renders the same in every renderer (probe: 9/9 labels of 24/32/40 pt within tolerance, versus 4.5–8 pt low for a 1.2× multiplier on two lines). A 1.2× multiplier adds the extra space above the line in PowerPoint-like renderers (label drifts 3–6 pt low at 24–44 pt) but not in Keynote (label sits high), so any offset measured in one renderer is wrong in the other; at 1.0 both center the line box, and a 12-label probe measured within tolerance in Artifact Tool. Apply `opticalOffsetY` only for a residual measured in the user's target renderer, say which renderer it was, and never to compensate for 1.2× on a single line. For a reproducible font/renderer offset, retain native middle alignment and apply a measured vertical inset correction with `box(..., {opticalOffsetY: value})` in source PPT points. Positive values move the visible label down. This is an explicit, measured exception to symmetric top/bottom insets; left/right remain symmetric. The helper records the offset in the object name so the structural checker can verify it. Do not hard-code one offset for every font, size or multiline label. Run `scripts/check_slide_conventions.py` for explicit centering, symmetric insets, and separate-overlay detection. This geometric check cannot replace the rendered check. Use `prose:` as the name prefix only for intentional paragraph containers, never to bypass checks on node labels.

### Final optical check

Measure actual visible glyph ink rather than the text frame, line box or `anchor=ctr` setting. Run `scripts/check_rendered_node_alignment.py` against the exported PPTX and matching final native-PDF PNGs, then inspect the flagged crops and critical molecular nodes at slide size and close view. The script compares dark label ink bounds with each unrotated top-level node center; colored text, grouped nodes, clipping and overlaps require manual review. Its 0.08em threshold is a review tolerance, not an aesthetic score. Apply a measured correction to related labels sharing the same typography when warranted, export again, and recheck the final renderer. Record the measured before/after offsets and final crop locations in QA. A structural centering pass alone must never be reported as visually centered.

For paired columns such as rank/name or label/value, align corresponding rows on shared baselines. Two independent multiline text boxes with different font sizes have different line pitches even when their top edges align. Use individual row objects or a suitable native table, and inspect the final last row as well as the first; do not compensate with spaces or arbitrary paragraph gaps.

### Shapes in biological mechanisms

For this user's mechanism slides, avoid making every actor and state an identical sharp rectangle. Use a restrained semantic vocabulary: an ellipse or softly rounded shape for an enzyme, a capsule or compact labelled circle for a small molecule/phosphate when appropriate, and a distinct substrate representation that persists through its states. Keep the same actor's shape and color consistent across active/inhibited panels. This is a readable schematic, not a claim about molecular geometry; do not invent protein surfaces or add unlabelled decorative circles. Ordinary result text can remain unboxed. Rounded forms must still fit their labels with optical centering.

## Composition recipes (dissertation anatomy, enforced by `scripts/check_layout_quality.py`)

Every content slide has the same six zones: gray section label → bold black title → gray subtitle → **evidence band** (`BAND`: x 95, y 285, 1730 × 555, captions included) → **closing interpretation line** (`closing()`, y 872) → reference footer (lower right, ≤ 2 rows, `references()`) with the gray page number centered below. Use the helpers in `assets/style-kit.mjs` (copy and read `assets/kit-demo.mjs`); they already carry the dissertation palette (title blue `#1F77B4`, emphasis red `#E02000`, sky `#00A0F0`, green `#10B000`, orange `#F07000`, brown `#B05000`, purple `#9966CC`).

- **One hero figure per slide.** The evidence object takes ≥ 55 % of the band and at most three secondary text/stat blocks sit beside it; four or more data blocks means split the slide or promote one block (a tile dashboard of five statistics is not a slide). Panels in a row share top edge, plot baseline, caption row and right edge; a single object (a model box) sits on its neighbours' centre line; headers use the same x in every row.
- **Text beside a reused figure.** A text column next to a pasted paper figure carries one short statement (≈ 12 words) at most; the figure takes ≥ 60 % of the content width. Outlined text boxes keep at least half a line of padding on every side and ≥ 40 pt before the next block. Credits ("Collaborating with …") go in a corner slot (photo + name as in the dissertation), never inline between body and closing line. State a claim once: the statement line, the numbered lines and the closing line must not restate the same sentence. Section labels are short noun phrases (≤ 4 words); red marks one phrase per text block.
- **Header position.** Section label, title and subtitle are single lines at line spacing 1.0 (the kit does this): with 1.2× the first line sits 6–17 pt lower than the dissertation in PowerPoint-like renders.
- **When 55 % is impossible.** A figure that is tall or square beside text (an architecture diagram, a 1.35:1 panel set) spans the full band height and takes the width it needs, at least half the band area; its smallest internal label must stay readable at slide scale (≈ 14 pt equivalent). If it is not, crop it to the panel under discussion (and show the other panels on their own slide or in the appendix) instead of keeping a whole figure with 9 pt labels.
- **Reserved bottom band.** The closing line ends ≥ 40 px above the reference footer (a 2-row footer means a one-line closing; a 2-line closing needs a footer of one row or point-of-use markers only); the footer is ≤ 2 rows (`references()`), ends above the page-number row and never wraps around the page number. More sources than fit means markers at the point of use plus a shorter footer, or a split slide.
- **Two text tiers under a figure.** A figure's own label (bold, 30–34 pt) and one gray caption/value line (28–30 pt). A third tier, bold accent value lines that outrank the structure, or a stat list under the figure means the slide has two messages; move the extra to notes or split the slide. Rows in a list and rows in an adjacent table share one pitch.
- **One shape, one arrow, one hue per meaning, deck-wide.** Enzyme/model = ellipse (`stage(..., {geometry:'ellipse'})`), small molecule = capsule (`borderRadius:'rounded-full'`), residue/site = circle, data/step = rectangle — in a mechanism or pipeline diagram do not draw every node as the same sharp rectangle; one arrow style per diagram (thin arrows; block arrows only for stage jumps); a hue that means “hypothesis” or “unverified” is never reused for a result; numbered lists use the category colors consistently.
- **One interpretation device per slide.** Use a plain statement line *or* numbered lines *or* the closing line for the message, not several; the figure stays clear of text boxes.
- **Proofread pasted raster text.** Read every label in a reused figure at full size; a typo baked into the author's picture cannot be fixed in the PPTX, so crop it out or redraw that panel natively. When the brief or the user says to reuse the figure unchanged, the brief wins: keep it, list the typo in the notes and name it in the delivery message so the user can fix the source figure.
- **Subtitle vs closing line.** If the author's own subtitle and closing line say the same thing, keep the closing line (it carries the slide) and rewrite the subtitle as the topic/method noun phrase; a reference marker goes in the subtitle as `[[ref|[1]]]` (the kit's `slide()` renders it small and gray). A closing line holds about 74 Latin characters or 41 Hangul per line and at most two lines; `closing()` moves itself up to y 852 / h 96 for two lines, so break it by meaning with `\n`.
- **Titles are the brief's, verbatim.** A Korean deck keeps the English topic/method titles the user supplies (dissertation style: “Top-ranked candidates”); do not translate them. Korean goes in the subtitle, labels and closing line.
- **True counts on a log or rank chart.** `barChart()` has no per-bar label override and QuickLook drops custom data labels: put the count or rank into the category label (“식물 연결 111,084 (24.1%)”) so the number survives every renderer.
- **Numbered findings and the closing line.** A conclusion slide may carry numbered findings plus one closing line (the user approved this on the Korean conclusion slide); the layout checker exempts numbered-findings slides from `no-takeaway` but the closing line is still welcome. Keep one emphasis colour phrase per item at most.
- **Closing lines assert; disclaimers go to the notes.** In r20 six of nine closing lines ended on a negation or disclaimer ("직접 근거 아님", "…의미 아님"). State what the figure shows; keep a caveat on the slide only when it changes how the figure is read (e.g. "not docked", "assay conditions differ"), and keep its strength equal to the brief's (감소 stays 감소, never 없음).
- **Inhibition is drawn as blocked flow.** A normal arrowhead plus a floating word reads as activation: end the stub with a red × (native shape) and attach the label to it; keep the enzyme's colour and weight the same in active and inhibited lanes.
- **Do not mix a partition and nested filters in one bar series.** 111,084 + 349,521 = 460,605 is a split; 7,225 → 2,744 → 90 are successive filters. Draw the filters as a funnel (stage cards with counts and the filter named) or as two charts.
- **Chart rows share a plot box.** Same panel height, same axis-title presence and similar category-label length per row, otherwise the baselines drift (r16/r20: x-axes at y 670/681/713). The kit's theme colour scheme makes any default series/marker the accent blue or a gray.
- **When the brief carries more than the slide can hold.** Follow the tie-breaker order (closing line, numbered items, one statement, body); the author's remaining sentences and any panel the brief names but you drop (e.g. panels b and c of a wide figure) go to the notes, and the delivery message lists exactly what moved so the user can overrule. A label-style author closing ("Comparison to …") becomes the subtitle; the closing line asserts.
- **Value axes.** Panels of one metric share one axis range (the largest value rounded up); the unit and direction sit in the gray panel heading because horizontal bars show no axis title in every renderer.
- **References without a journal.** For records with no journal (preprints, model cards) print author and year (and the arXiv ID); never invent a venue; if the ID implies a different year than the record, note it in the notes.
- **Tables.** A table from the clean template (full grid borders, top-anchored text) is overridden: use `table()` (rules only, vertically centred; `headerAnchor:'bottom'` for tall headers). The closing line stays at y 872 on every slide (y 852 only when it needs two lines), footer or not.
- **More than two footer rows.** `references()` accepts two rows: abbreviate journals first, then merge the sources of one slide's compound drawings into one entry ("Name [1] · 구조 [11]"), and leave dataset snapshots (knowledge-graph export, ChEMBL release) to the notes with their locators.
- **Cite only what the slide claims.** When the brief forbids a caveat of a source on the slide (e.g. an α/β distinction), cite the source for the narrow claim it supports and keep the caveat in the notes.
- **Line-art slides and the fill floor.** A slide of structures or diagrams that fails `sparse` is fixed by enlarging the figures or adding the explanatory object the slide lacks (a labelled scaffold panel), never by adding evidence that is not in the brief.
- **Conclusion slide title.** Bold black and left-aligned like every other content slide (blue titles are for cover and divider slides only); the style-reference README's blue conclusion title does not apply, and the empty section-label box is not left behind.
- **Flow charts and pipelines (user liked this look, 2026-10-02).** Build them from `stageCard()` (small-caps kicker, bold name, one or two gray detail lines), thin attached connectors `link()` with a short verb label `linkLabel()` ("asks", "calls", "returns"), and `loopBack()` for a return path (three straight connectors through invisible waypoints; the loop gets its own label in the accent colour). Card state carries information: `done` (light-gray fill, black outline), `active` (white, black outline), `planned` (white, thin gray outline, muted text), and `statusPill()` under a card says "Done / In test / Planned". One accent colour at most (the active loop), step numbers only when order matters. No block arrows, no curved or elbow connectors (Apple's renderer collapses routed connectors to a straight line; the three-segment `loopBack()` renders everywhere), no emoji or clip-art icons. Keep each card line under ~24 characters in a 380 pt card (`\n` in `detail` breaks lines).
- **SVG and other vector pictures.** `slide.images.add({svg})` embeds an SVG, but Artifact Tool writes a blank placeholder PNG as the fallback, so Keynote, QuickLook and PDF export show an empty box; only PowerPoint 365 draws it (and can "Convert to Shape"). Do not use it for diagrams. Draw flow charts with native shapes and connectors (editable everywhere); for a complex illustration render the SVG to a high-resolution PNG and insert that (not editable). A hybrid SVG + real PNG fallback would need a post-processing step that the skill does not have.
- **Charts with markers.** Category labels that carry reference markers (`DiffDock-L [3]`) at ≥ 24 pt do not fit three column charts side by side: use horizontal bars.
- **No cover slide.** The first content slide's gray section label names the target and objective (for example “RDBDock · Motivation”); do not add a cover just to say it.
- **Compact footer, markers where the claim is.** Put markers in the chart's category labels (“DiffDock-L [3]”; the marker checker reads chart text) or on panel titles, and keep the footer to abbreviated entries; do not repeat the same sources in a gray “Peers: …” line. Grade peer series in light-to-mid grays and keep the proposed series accent; one label system per figure (all inside or all outside), and use a hue for one meaning only.
- **Annotations on clear background.** Residue labels, leaders and citation markers sit on white (`figureLabel()` has a white halo) or beside the ribbon, never on ribbon edges or structure pixels; structure insets are ≥ 480×360 px with the ligand fully inside the frame. The closing line states the evidence's message; it neither repeats the subtitle nor is a caveat.
- **No blockquote/callout styles, ever.** The user never wants quote-style boxes: no cream/pastel panel behind a statement, no colored side bar next to text, no shaded “key message” box, no “next slide” or “note” boxes. State the message as plain black text (red/bold on one or two phrases) or in the closing line. The dissertation PPTX itself has such a box on 4 of 107 slides (6, 89, 104, 105); that is not a precedent for new decks. `check_layout_quality.py` flags them.
- **Fills are white or neutral gray.** In the dissertation PPTX the shape fills are white (84), #EBEBEB (55), #DDDDDD, #CCCCCC, #F7F7F7 and black; colour is carried by text and thin outlines. Do not tint boxes pastel blue/cream/pink; a node's meaning comes from its shape and outline/text colour. Small saturated markers (≤ 100 pt circles such as phosphate chips) and chart series fills are the exceptions.
- **Evidence dominates.** The figure/diagram/chart takes ≥ 55 % of the band; text columns take at most a third of the width. Crop rasters to the object (auto-trim white margins, keep ~2 % padding) so no picture edge cuts through a cartoon, drawing or label. A slide whose figure is small beside a text wall is rebuilt, not padded.
- **Closing line on every slide.** One centered black ~40 pt line (two at most) that states what to take away or hands off to the next slide, with at most two `[[red|…]]` key phrases. It replaces gray hedging captions and left-aligned blue lines. Put at most one short qualifier on a slide (inside the closing line or a caption); everything else belongs in notes.
- **Plain colour budget (user, 2026-10-01: “색을 쓸데없이 많이 사용” — three category colours on the Korean conclusion slide were pointless).** Text is black and gray. Per slide, **at most two chromatic hue families**: the accent blue (title, the proposed method, finding numerals) and red for one or two key phrases. Do not colour list numerals/labels by category, do not add orange/green/purple for decoration, do not recolour the same entity differently on neighbouring slides. A second or third hue is allowed only when categories must be told apart on that slide (a mechanism with active vs blocked lanes, a structure figure's residue label), and then it keeps its meaning across the deck. `check_layout_quality.py` counts hue families per slide (`too-many-colors`; `--max-hues 3` for a justified exception) and red emphasis runs (`red-overuse`, more than two per slide). Style reference the user named: the design system at https://claude.ai/artifact/8yFPceWkL7KQmN211Wkgkp (its README is the brand book; where its conclusion slide uses three category colours, this plain rule wins).
- **Emphasis system.** Bold red marks the key phrase of a sentence (max two per slide); blue/green/orange/purple mark meaningful categories and keep their meaning across the deck. Never set whole lines in accent blue.
- **Diagram vocabulary.** Process steps are `stage()` boxes: thin gray outline and gray text when passive, thick black outline when it is the step being discussed; arrows are slim gray `thinArrow()`/connectors (blue only to mark the path of interest). No heavy black block arrows, no pale-blue filled boxes. Mechanism slides keep the rounded/elliptical actor vocabulary below.
- **Ellipses hold less text than their box.** Text fits in about 72 % of an ellipse's width (a label that touches the ellipse edge looks like it spilled out); widen the ellipse or shorten the label. `box()` warns and `check_layout_quality.py` flags `label-overflow`.
- **No empty boxes.** A diagram node is sized to its text (≤ ~2.5× the text area). A black-box model is a compact node with a one-line descriptor, or a `stageCard()` (gray small-caps kicker, bold name, detail line) like the dissertation's pipeline boxes — never a large rectangle holding one word. Sibling boxes in a diagram do not all share one sharp-rectangle shape when they mean different things (data = rectangle, model/enzyme = ellipse or rounded).
- **Structure highlights are quiet.** Chemistry/scaffold highlights use a pale tint with a thin halo (#CFE3F5 or #FFE0C2 under standard atom colors), not saturated cyan 14 px strokes; check that no ring atom or label is clipped by the image edge.
- **Non-evidence ink ≤ one third.** Notes, KPI numerals, captions and citations together take at most about a third of the content band; a slide that reads like a dashboard (small chart + big numerals + gray notes + a long footer) is split or reduced to one claim. No hero numerals (a giant “6” is not in the dissertation's vocabulary); a rank or count belongs in the label or the closing line.
- **Flow diagrams use the whole band.** Scale a pipeline/mechanism to the full content band with balanced left/right ink and no dead side strips; use the dissertation's box grammar: heavy black outline = this work (or the step under discussion), thin gray = external/other, one thin gray arrow style per diagram; do not invert it (a black box for an unknown/external part).
- **The closing line says only what the figure shows**, and emphasis colour comes from the figure (Val135 purple in the image → purple/red emphasis in the line, not green). Drop a claim the figure cannot support (“selectivity verified”).
- **Chart rows share geometry.** Panels in a row share plot top and baseline and one accent blue (no teal beside blue); rank charts carry a direction cue (“lower is better”); centre labels on their nodes (a label 20 px off its node is a defect).
- **Recipes by job:** big figure (+ one plain statement line above or below it); two-panel comparison (equal panels, same camera/scale, bold panel title above, its value below it); pipeline (`stage()` + `thinArrow()`, external-vs-ours labels in category colors under the boxes); benchmark (native chart fills the band, proposed series accent blue, comparators gray, values on bars); table/case (thin separators, bold gray header, native table); text-only talking points and conclusions (`findings()`).
- **Tie-breakers when the brief supplies more text than the slide can carry:** keep, in this order, the closing line, numbered items, one statement line, then body sentences; the rest goes to the notes. Cropping a multi-panel paper figure to the panel(s) the presenter will explain is acceptable ("reuse unchanged" means no redrawing or editing of that panel, not "show every panel"); name the dropped panels in the notes. With no cover slide, slide 1's section label or subtitle carries the target's name; do not add content the brief does not contain.
- **Inline markers are small and gray.** In a sentence, write the reference as `[[ref|[3]]]` (regular gray at 0.75× the text size), never as full-size bold black text; crop a pasted multi-panel figure tightly enough to drop its panel letter (a stray “a”).
- **Glyphs outside the face.** If the deck font lacks a character (run `scripts/check_font_glyphs.py`; Helvetica Neue lacks “→”), do not type it: use a native arrow shape between the items, or rephrase (“a: b”). A silent fallback glyph breaks the one-font-family rule.
- **The approved brief outranks the budget below.** Content the brief requires (per-database counts, three panels on one slide) stays: compress it into one caption line, a small table or a compact panel row, and drop optional detail first.
- **Content budget.** One message per slide; beside the hero figure show at most three numbers, and move the rest (per-database counts, intermediate statistics, atom-pair distances, confidence intervals) to notes or a supplementary slide. Hedging or verification-status text is limited to one short qualifier that changes interpretation; others go to notes. Entities are named for the audience (“Candidate 1”, a compound name), never bare InChIKeys on the slide (identifiers go in notes). Reuse the same example compound/structure across consecutive slides so the story reads as one thread; a bar/count graphic that encodes numbers is a native chart or table with its data, not hand-drawn shapes.
- **Charts.** Artifact Tool has no log axis: plot log10 values and label the true counts, saying so in the axis line; keep plotted values to ≤ 15 digits (round to 4 decimals); set an explicit typeface on every data-label/axis override (the default exports as Calibri and fails the font policy); `yAxis.visible:false` is ignored. Use `barChart()` / `scatterChart()` from the style kit (one-decimal labels via `valuesFormatCode`, one data series only so the workbook holds real values, the main bar accent via a per-point fill, rest gray, text ≥ 24 pt, explicit marker fill, horizontal bars reversed so they read top-to-bottom; the renderer does not draw value-axis titles on horizontal bars, so repeat the unit in a gray panel line). Axis titles with units; say when bars are cumulative or binned; add a baseline/chance reference when it matters; all chart text ≥ 24 pt including ticks and captions (no 22 pt “caption tier”; `check_layout_quality.py` rejects runs under 24 pt); main series accent, others gray; native chart with data.
- **Each figure carries the slide's claim.** A structure, schematic or panel that does not support the slide's message is cut or replaced by one that does (an unrelated protein ribbon beside a biology claim is decoration). State the target/objective by name on the first slide and let each closing line hand off to the next.
- **Mechanism lanes.** For active vs inhibited states draw two lanes sharing one substrate row: the same substrate in each state, the blocked step marked at the same site with the blocker drawn there, the downstream consequence of each lane at its end. A ligand name or caption alone does not show competition.

## Choose visuals for the explanation

Start with what the presenter needs to explain and what the audience should understand. In this user's biology talks, molecular structure, binding interactions and biochemical reactions are the preferred explanatory entry points. Do not substitute a microscopy, histology or cell-assay panel merely because it is available in a cited paper. Use such a panel when the experimental comparison is itself essential and its specimen, intervention, readout and conclusion can be explained clearly. Otherwise retain that biological evidence and its citation in concise slide text or notes while giving visual space to a relevant structural or mechanistic explanation. A structure explains a molecular interaction; it does not independently establish hair growth, cell proliferation or clinical efficacy.

When the user explicitly requests a structural explanation, a flowchart of protein names does not satisfy that request. Include an explanatory structural view or a chemically explicit site/interaction representation, with identified components and its source. For the current GSK3B biology introduction, connect the molecular interaction relevant to the stated biological rationale to the literature evidence; a set of β-catenin/TCF/FGF text boxes alone is insufficient. A suitable available coordinate source may support this role, but no particular PDB or fixed layout is mandatory.

For mechanisms, prefer an editable schematic showing the participating molecules, relevant sites or compartments, before/after states and causal transitions. Compare active and inhibited states when that is the question. Label what changes at each arrow: binding/competition, phosphate transfer, recognition, degradation or localization. A list of sentences connected by arrows is not enough if the molecular actors and changed states remain invisible. Integrate a PDB pocket view as a structural anchor when useful, rather than placing one static whole-protein image beside an unrelated prose flow. A schematic is a static figure too; animation is optional, not the meaning of this preference.

In a reaction sequence, depict the same substrate across its changed states; naming successive enzymes is not a substitute. For phosphorylation/degradation, mark the relevant substrate sites and their unmodified/phosphorylated state, then recognition/tagging and the substrate fate. Mark the point where inhibition changes the route. An enzyme-name strip labelled “CK1 → GSK3 → β-TrCP → proteasome” with a concluding sentence does not show these substrate states. Inspect the actual mechanism slide against this requirement before treating the visual as complete.

For example, an ATP-competitive inhibition explanation can connect ATP-site occupancy and substrate phosphorylation to the downstream fate of the substrate, with separate active/inhibited states. Render any claimed bound geometry from real coordinates. A schematic substrate, phosphate symbol or compartment is an explanatory element, not a claimed experimental complex. Preserve evidence boundaries, source markers and uncertainty at the relevant steps. Do not add docking or fabricated co-complex geometry to make the scheme more elaborate.

Meaningful schematics are preferred over decorative symbols. Use conventional, labelled molecular/state representations and native arrows/connectors. Do not use unexplained ladders, dots or pseudo-molecules for gene expression, proliferation or growth signals. Avoid an automatic rule that either paper images or schematics always win; choose according to the explanatory job.

Inspect original papers for useful panels when an experimental image or plot serves that job. Retrieve a readable original, preserve axes, scale bars, labels and legend context, and retain DOI/URL, panel number, species/assay conditions and crop/adaptation details. Record reuse/license information when available and follow applicable terms. Do not erase attribution. A download failure calls for another source route or a supported alternative, not an unclear placeholder.

For protein structures, binding pockets and complexes, use experimental PDB/mmCIF coordinates and PyMOL, following `molecular-visualization.md`. Use native editable objects for explanatory states, reaction relationships and labels. Retain generation provenance when used.
