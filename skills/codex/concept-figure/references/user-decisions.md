Dated decisions of the user behind this skill's rules, in the user's own words. Read this file before editing the skill or when the origin or scope of a rule is in doubt. Add each new decision here with its date; never drop or narrow an entry without asking the user.

In the quotes below `[내 논문]` stands for a project name, removed from this public file at the user's request on 2026-11-14 ("이름 빼고 ㄱㄱ").

- 2026-11-13 (ChatGPT app, Claude chat 01a1d32f-0ea3-7131-82df-048fbf3e733a): the request that created this skill. User's words: "nature style 의 figure 들 그리는 nature-figure 는 사실 결과같은거 정리할 때만 쓰여. 근데 fig 1 같은 경우는 보통 대부분 concept figure, 모델 그림들 이런게 들어가는데, 그런것들도 예쁘게 그릴 수 있는 skill 을 만들고 싶어. ML conference paper 들에서 좋은 reference 들 찾아서 해당 그림들을 그릴 수 있도록 해줘. 편집가능해야하기 때문에 기본적으로 html 작업 → png 나 이런거로 변환 이런 식으로 해야할듯." → concept and model figures are this skill's job and result plots stay with `nature-figure`; the patterns come from ML conference papers (`archetypes.md`); the editable source is HTML and PDF/PNG are rendered from it.

User's words: "점수화 기준을 만들어서 계속 개선해나가고 싶음. 지금도 나쁘진 않은데 진짜 예쁜 figure 들을 만들고 싶어서그럼. concept figure 같은걸 사실 그릴 때 컨셉 잡는게 좀 어려운거같기도하고. 그런것도 같이 하면 좋겠는데. [내 논문] figure 1 같은 스타일로도 나올 수 있는지 참고해봐. 내용은 절대보면 안돼." → (1) figures are scored with `references/rubric.md` and `scripts/score.py` and revised against the largest losses; (2) the skill helps find the concept before drawing (`references/concept-development.md`, thumbnails first); (3) the Fig. 1 of the user's own manuscript is a reference for what the user finds pretty, to be looked at, never a template (see the correction of the same day below); (4) the content of that figure is never read by the drawing agent: its style came from numeric statistics printed by a script and from a style-only report by a delegated reader (Claude Sonnet 5.5), and any later comparison with it goes the same way.
- 2026-11-13 (same chat), on the kit sheet: "기호들이 중앙 배열이 안 되어 있음. 좀 더 내려야 중앙에 배치될듯." (the +, × and σ in `.op` circles) → symbols in circles, chips and cells are centred on their ink (`centreInk` in `figure.js`), the layout is zoomed eight times so that Chrome's pixel snapping no longer lifts small text, and `scripts/ink_check.py` measures the painted result (0.06 pt after the fix, about 0.7 pt before).
- 2026-11-13 (same chat), on the curve specimens: "화살표가 너무 어색해. 그 실선, 점선이 좀 더 curvature 가 커야지 자연스럽게 보일듯?" → a curve between two ends on one side is a round bow that leaves and arrives at an angle, with `bend` as its height; the old shape left at a right angle and ran flat.
- 2026-11-13 (same chat), on the example drawn in the manner of that figure: "이건 너무 [내 논문] 스러운 게 되어 버린거같은데, 걍 예쁘게 하는게 제일 중요하고 굳이 그 양식을 복사 붙여넣기 하듯이 하라는 뜻은아니었어." → prettiness is the aim; a reference figure, the user's own included, lends principles (restraint, white space, few colours, one consistent language) and never its palette, components or layout. The palette values, stroke constants and card styling taken from that figure were removed from the kit, the example that imitated it was deleted, and the rubric no longer uses that figure as its anchor. Do not measure "similarity to" any one figure as a goal.
- 2026-11-13 (same chat), while the round-8 examples were being revised: "쓸데 없이 많은 여백은 감점요소야." → white space is for separating parts and nothing else; space beyond that loses points. `figure.js` measures it inside the drawn area as `slack` (the share farther than 6 pt from anything drawn) and as the largest empty rectangle, `render.py` warns above 24 % and 12 % (`empty-space`), `score.py` gives the two 8 of the 40 measured points, and criterion C3 of the rubric names it. Measured the same way from the papers, CLIP Fig. 1, DiT Fig. 3 and I-JEPA Fig. 2 have 11 to 15 % slack and MoCo Fig. 2 and ViT Fig. 1 about 29 %. This bounds the "white space" named in the entry above: it means calm separation, not empty area.
- 2026-11-13 (same chat), on preview v5: "이제는 여백이 문제가 되는건 없는듯?" → the white-space level of round 8 (6 to 21 % slack) is accepted as not a problem; keep it there or tighter.
- 2026-11-13 (same chat), on the thumbnails example, which had rendered with no errors or warnings: "글자랑 이미지들이 좀 겹치고 화살표가 c 에서 이상하네. 이런 문제는 절대 안생기게 검수하는 과정도 skill 에 넣거나 하는게 좋지 않을지?" → an inspection gate is part of the workflow (`references/inspection.md`): `render.py` writes zoomed tiles and an unticked receipt, every tile is looked at against a fixed checklist before a figure is scored or shown, a second reader repeats it before a final delivery, `score.py` marks an unticked figure NOT INSPECTED, and `check_examples.py` re-renders every example after a kit change and names the ones that changed. The defects seen became checks: `wire-leg`, `on-rule`, `panel-overflow`.
